How to pick and answer for this category

TV characters change over time. A character in season one can be quite different by season six, so pick a point in the story before you start and answer for that version. This matters most for characters whose morals shift, such as Walter White and Saul Goodman, or whose role changes, such as Eleven.

Remember too that TV characters are often less universally known than movie stars. The Genie asks whether your character is known to almost everyone, and an honest No for a cult favorite like Omar Little is the right answer. For long-running figures such as The Doctor or Sherlock Holmes, remember that many actors have played them; answer for the version in your head.

Why television characters are harder than film characters

A film gives a character two hours and one finished arc. A long-running series gives them years, several writers and a personality that can change between seasons. That is the main reason television characters sit closer to each other in the trait data: they share settings, they share tone, and they often share a profession or a role that the questions can see. Two characters from the same show may differ mainly in temperament, which is the hardest thing to ask about in a yes-or-no format.

The practical consequence is that season matters. A character who begins as a subordinate and ends as a leader will answer the leadership question differently depending on where you are in the story, and a round that mixes the two will stall.
