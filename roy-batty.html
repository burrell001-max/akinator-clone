Roy Batty: A Character Guide for MindGenie AI

By Kent B Williams · Published November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Roy Batty is a villain by role and a victim by design, and the engine stores both: fictional, superhuman, a villain, with a nemesis, a transformation and a partial on humanity. He is one of the few roster entries whose stored answers are as ambiguous as the film.

Why the round takes a while

His clean answers are strong but not unique: fictional, superhuman, villainous, with an enemy and a transformation. That could describe several science-fiction entries.

The partials are what slow it down. Humanity, science, weapon and team are all stored in the middle because he is a manufactured being who leads a small group and fights without a signature weapon.

Who he is

A combat model replicant who returns to Earth to force his maker to extend his life, and fails. He kills without hesitation and then saves the man hunting him, which is the scene everyone remembers.

The engine records him as a villain because that is his function in the story, not because the film is willing to call him one.

Where players get tripped up

The human question. He looks human and is explicitly not, and the engine stores a partial rather than a clean no, which keeps him alive beside androids and humans alike.

The fame question is a no. He is famous among film fans, not to almost everyone, and a yes pushes the round towards blockbuster villains.

The speech was not in the script

Rutger Hauer rewrote the final monologue himself, trimming the original and adding the line about tears in rain. It is the reason the character is remembered as tragic rather than merely dangerous.

Philip K. Dick's novel has a different replicant plot and a different ending, and the film changes the question from empathy to memory. The engine records the film version, which is the one players mean.
