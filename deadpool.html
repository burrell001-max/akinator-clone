⚔️ Deadpool

By Kent B Williams · Published 1 November 2026

Deadpool is Wade Wilson, a wisecracking mercenary in a red suit who is famous for his regenerative healing and for talking directly to the audience. He is an anti-hero rather than a traditional good guy, and his violent comedy sets him apart from most Marvel characters.

Why the Genie sometimes needs a few extra questions

Deadpool takes a little more work because his answers are often mixed: he is neither clearly a hero nor a villain, and he overlaps with several masked characters. Questions about humor, violence, and the fourth wall are not part of the Genie's usual list, so it relies on mask, powers, and weapons.

Background on Deadpool

Deadpool was created by Rob Liefeld and Fabian Nicieza and first appeared in New Mutants #98 in 1991. Wade Wilson is a former soldier who is diagnosed with cancer, undergoes an experimental treatment, and gains a powerful healing factor that also leaves him badly scarred. He becomes a mercenary and wears a red-and-black mask to hide his appearance.

He is best known for breaking the fourth wall, speaking to readers and viewers directly, and for his comic-book violence. Ryan Reynolds played the character in Deadpool (2016), Deadpool 2 (2018), and Deadpool & Wolverine (2024), which made him a mainstream star. His signature gear includes katanas and pistols.

Traits that give them away

  • Regenerative healing. He recovers from nearly any injury, which makes the powers question a yes.
  • Red mask and suit. A defining visual that makes the mask question a yes.
  • Katanas and guns. Weapons are central to how he fights.
  • Anti-hero. Heroism and villainy questions are mixed, so 'probably' answers are common.
  • Fourth-wall humor. He talks to the audience, which sets him apart from other masked characters.

How a round usually runs

A Deadpool round usually takes a question or two more than the biggest names, because his answers are mixed by design. He has powers, he wears a mask, he uses weapons, and he is neither a clean hero nor a clean villain, so the engine has to find the combination instead of one obvious trait.

Easy to confuse with

Deadpool is most often confused with Spider-Man, since both wear red-and-black masks and joke constantly. Wolverine is another frequent mix-up because they share a healing factor and appear together in recent films. Harley Quinn and the Joker are comedic, chaotic figures but come from the DC universe. The weapon question and the 'sometimes a hero' answers separate him from Spider-Man.

Tips for playing as Deadpool

Because Deadpool is neither a clear hero nor a villain, use the 'probably' options instead of forcing a yes or no. That gives the Genie a more accurate picture. For a harder round, think of his friends or enemies from the same stories.

When to pick Deadpool

Pick Deadpool when you want to see what happens when a character refuses to fit one column. He is also a good pick if you want to practise the middle answers, because forcing him into yes or no is exactly what makes a round go wrong.

Answers that go wrong

The hero and villain questions are the usual problem. He is an anti-hero, so a confident yes to either contradicts half of what the engine holds. Answering probably to both, and yes to mask and weapons, gives a much cleaner picture.

Frequently asked questions

Does Deadpool have superpowers?

Yes. His healing factor is the ability the engine records, and it is what lets him survive injuries that would end most characters.

Why is he hard to place as a hero?

Because he is written as an anti-hero. He does violent work for money and still does the right thing often enough that neither label fits cleanly.

Is Deadpool connected to Spider-Man?

Only through shared traits. Both wear masks and joke while they fight, so vague answers can drift from one to the other.

What makes his round end correctly?

The weapons and the healing. A yes to both, with cautious answers on morality, is usually enough.
