Cortana: A Hero the Data Keeps Confusing With a Villain

By Kent B Williams · Updated November 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Cortana is the mirror image of GLaDOS in the engine's data: the same artificial intelligence, the same science background, the same mind-over-strength profile, the same lack of a weapon — and the opposite alignment. Because the two are so close, her rounds are decided by three questions and almost nothing else.

Seven yeses, four partials and a lot of nos

Cortana's sheet is the most hedged in the roster after Thanos, and the hedges are all in interesting places:

  • Yes: fictional, powered, a hero, science and technology, mind over strength, speaks, real name public.
  • Partly: part of a team, known to almost everyone, has a nemesis, undergoes a transformation.
  • No: human, underwater, mask or costume, from space, a villain, animated series, signature weapon, famous sidekick, royalty, from a book, fantasy world, school, mentor, romance, sea voyage, paying job, sole protagonist.

Those four partials are the engine's honesty. Cortana works with the UNSC but is not a unit in it. She is famous among people who play games rather than famous the way Batman is. She has antagonists rather than one arch-enemy. And she deteriorates rather than transforms — the sheet refuses to call rampancy a clean change of state.

GLaDOS at 0.161, and the three answers that settle it

The two AIs agree on twenty-two of twenty-eight traits, which is the tightest non-identical pair in the game-character pool. They split on six:

  • Hero: Cortana yes, GLaDOS no.
  • Villain: Cortana no, GLaDOS yes.
  • Team or organisation: Cortana partly, GLaDOS no.
  • Ruling: Cortana no, GLaDOS yes.
  • Nemesis: Cortana partly, GLaDOS yes.
  • Transformation: Cortana partly, GLaDOS no.

Notice what is missing: every question about species, powers, weapons, speech, recognition and setting is a tie. There is no way to separate them by asking what they are. You separate them by asking what they do — help or harm, command or serve, change or persist.

This is the single most useful thing to know about playing Cortana. If a round has reached two non-human, unarmed, science-literate, mind-first characters, it is almost certainly these two, and the next question is the hero question.

The second tier: L and Tesla at 0.232

After GLaDOS the distance jumps. L, the detective from Death Note, is 0.232 away, and the split is almost entirely about humanity and fiction: L is a human in a non-fantasy world, Cortana is neither. The remaining differences are about teams, books and transformation, which the engine uses to keep the two apart when a round reaches "mind over strength" early.

Nikola Tesla sits at the same distance from the other direction — a real person with a science trait and a public name, but no fiction, no powers, and a paying job. If a player answers "science" and "mind" but then says yes to human, the round leaves Cortana and heads for Tesla or L. That is the correct behaviour, and it is why the humanity answer matters so much.

Why "is your character human?" is the hinge

Players see a woman on screen and answer yes. Cortana is a smart AI, built from a cloned brain but not a person in the engine's sense: she has no body in the roster's terms, does not age, and cannot be described by any of the human traits.

Answering yes costs two things at once. It removes her from the AI pair entirely, and it pushes the round toward the human science characters — Tesla, L, Sherlock Holmes, Walter White — where a villain-or-hero answer then behaves unpredictably. Answering no keeps the AI corner intact and makes the rest of the round trivial.

The Halo timeline problem

Cortana's story runs from Combat Evolved in 2001 to Halo Infinite in 2021, and her role changes enormously across it: shipboard assistant, then fugitive, then leader of the Created, then something closer to a ghost. The television adaptation takes further liberties, including a physical body.

The sheet describes the games, and it describes the character across them rather than at one moment. That is why "villain" is a no even though the Created arc is antagonistic, and why "transformation" is only a partial even though rampancy is a change. If you are thinking of the television version, answer for the games anyway; the engine has no entry for a Cortana with a body.

What players get wrong, in order of frequency

  • Companion. Master Chief feels like the obvious answer to "famous sidekick", but he is not stored as one. The trait is about a sidekick attached to the character, not a partner the character supports.
  • From space. The Covenant are from space; Cortana is from a laboratory on Reach. The sheet marks space as a no, which is one of the cleanest splits from Thanos and Yoda.
  • Weapon. She controls ships and guns, but wielding is stored as a no. That is the same distinction that keeps GLaDOS unarmed.
  • Protagonist. She is a lead character and never the title-billed one. Answering yes here pushes the round toward Samus or Kratos.

Playing Cortana deliberately

  1. Answer no to human first. It is the answer that makes the round short.
  2. Answer yes to hero and no to villain. That single pair beats GLaDOS.
  3. Answer no to ruling. It is the second cleanest split from GLaDOS after alignment.
  4. Answer partly to team membership and partly to transformation, if the game offers you a middle answer.
  5. Expect the round to run to fourteen questions. Cortana is a medium pick because the AI corner is small, not because her round is short; the hero/villain split simply arrives late.
