Trinity: The Quietest Protagonist in the Roster

By Kent B Williams · Updated November 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Trinity is the hardest of the four renamed guides to play well, and not because her traits are exotic. She is a human fighter with a weapon, a team and a nemesis — a shape the roster has in abundance. What makes her round interesting is that the two characters nearest to her are a soldier from a stealth game and a desert raider from a post-apocalyptic film.

Seven yeses, seven partials, fourteen nos

Trinity's sheet is the most balanced in the roster: half the questions land in the middle. That is the engine being accurate about a character who is competent at many things and definitive about few:

  • Yes: fictional, a hero, human, wields a signature weapon, part of a team, has a nemesis, speaks.
  • Partly: powers, science and technology, known to almost everyone, famous companion, mind over strength, transformation, paying job.
  • No: ocean, mask or costume, from space, villain, animated series, royalty, from a book, fantasy world, school, mentor, romance, sea voyage, real name public, sole protagonist.

The last two nos are the interesting ones. Trinity is not the title-billed protagonist — Neo is — and her real name is not public, because "Trinity" is the only name the films give her.

Solid Snake at 0.125: the tightest match in her corner

Across all twenty-eight traits, Trinity's nearest neighbour is Solid Snake, at an average distance of 0.125. They agree on twenty-two and split on six:

  • Powers: Trinity partly, Snake no.
  • Mask or costume: Trinity no, Snake partly.
  • Team or organisation: Trinity yes, Snake partly.
  • Transformation: Trinity partly, Snake no.
  • Paying job: Trinity partly, Snake yes — a soldier for hire is employed.
  • Sole protagonist: Trinity no, Snake yes.

Read that list again and notice how thin the differences are: two characters who are both human fighters in teams, both armed, both with antagonists, both legendary among their own audiences and unknown outside them. The engine separates them with the powers and transformation answers, which in Trinity's case come from the Matrix itself.

Furiosa at 0.179, and the desert-fighter problem

Furiosa is the second neighbour and a cleaner contrast. They split on eight traits: powers, science, team membership, famous companion, royalty, mind over strength, real-name status and protagonist status.

That is the shape of the round Trinity forces you into. If you answer "part of a team" and "has a weapon" and "human", both women remain in play. What separates them is that Furiosa is the title character of her film and Trinity is not, that Furiosa has no powers at all, and that Furiosa's name — Imperator Furiosa — is the only name she is given too, which makes the real-name answer a trap in the opposite direction.

Eleven at 0.196 and the "known to everyone" answer

Eleven shares the team, the powers and the transformation, and differs on weapon, companion, school, fantasy world, romance and speech. The pair is worth knowing because both are famous partly through a single television or film moment rather than through a franchise of their own.

If a round has reached "human, team, weapon, nemesis" and is still open, the question that resolves it fastest is whether the character attends a school. Eleven does; Trinity never did.

The Matrix problem: what counts as a power

Inside the Matrix, Trinity can bend physics: she jumps between buildings, survives falls, and fights at a speed no human could manage. Outside it, she is a person in a chair with a jack in her skull. The sheet stores powers as a partial, which is the only defensible answer and also the reason her rounds are unstable.

Players who answer yes are describing Neo. Players who answer no are describing the real-world crew. The partial is correct, and using the middle answer when the interface offers it is the single biggest improvement most players can make for Trinity.

Misconceptions worth correcting before you play

  • Costume. The leather and sunglasses read as a uniform, but the sheet reserves "mask or costume" for characters whose identity is visually concealed. Trinity's is not.
  • Romance. Her relationship with Neo is central to the trilogy, and the sheet still stores no. The trait tracks a marriage or romance plot as the story's subject, which the Matrix films are not.
  • From a book. The films have tie-in comics and a shooting script published as a book; none of that makes her a book character.
  • Paying job. She is a hacker and an operator, which is work but not employment. Partly.
  • Protagonist. She is a lead. Neo is the One. No.

The 1999 film, the sequels and Resurrections

Trinity's role grows across Reloaded and Revolutions and returns in Resurrections, where the story is explicitly about her as much as about Neo. The sheet does not track which film; it describes the character as she appears across the series, which is why the protagonist answer stays no even though the fourth film argues otherwise.

If you are thinking of Resurrections specifically, answer for the trilogy's Trinity. The traits do not change.

Playing Trinity deliberately

  1. Answer yes to human, yes to weapon, yes to team. That trio puts you in the crowded fighter corner.
  2. Answer partly to powers and partly to transformation. Those two answers are what keep Snake out.
  3. Answer no to protagonist. It separates her from Furiosa faster than any other question.
  4. Answer no to school. Eleven is the character who would take the round otherwise.
  5. Expect fourteen questions. Trinity is filed under hard because the human-fighter corner is crowded, so the top of the ranking stays unsettled for most of the round.

For a harder version of the same round, think of Solid Snake and answer exactly as this page tells you. The engine should hold both for most of the round before the powers answer settles it.
