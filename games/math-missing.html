How to Play

Each equation has one value replaced by a box. Work out what belongs there, type it and press Enter. A correct answer scores a point and a new equation appears at once; a wrong one breaks your streak and shows the missing value for a moment before moving on. The round lasts sixty seconds from the moment you press Start.

The box is not always at the end. Sometimes it holds the sum, sometimes one of the numbers being added, sometimes the divisor, and sometimes the starting value. The operation symbols tell you which inverse to reach for: undo an addition with a subtraction, undo a multiplication with a division.

Rules and scoring

  • Answers are always whole numbers; nothing here produces a fraction or a negative.
  • Easy uses numbers up to 12, medium up to 20 with times tables to 12, hard up to 50 with multiplication to 15.
  • One point per correct answer, and the round ends when the timer hits zero.
  • Your best score for each difficulty is kept in this browser under mg-missing-best.
  • Enter submits; the input is refocused automatically after every question.

Solving the box quickly

Read the symbol before you read the numbers. If the equation is a sum with the box in the middle — 8 + ? = 21 — the answer is the difference, 13. If the box holds the total — 8 + 13 = ? — it is the sum. That distinction sounds obvious written down, but under a clock the eye tends to reach for the operation it sees, and adding when you should subtract is the single most common mistake in this game.

Multiplication and division follow the same rule with bigger gaps between the numbers. 7 × ? = 56 is a division question in disguise: eight sevens. ? ÷ 6 = 9 is a multiplication question in disguise: nine sixes. Since every division here is exact, the answer is always a whole number, and knowing that lets you rule out anything that would leave a remainder before you commit to typing it.
