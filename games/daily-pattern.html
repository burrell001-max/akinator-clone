How to Play

Five terms of a sequence are shown and the sixth is missing. Work out the rule, type the next term and press Enter. A correct answer turns the missing tile green and explains the rule; a wrong one shows the answer and moves on, because the puzzle set is fixed for the day and there is nothing to gain from hiding it.

All five sequences are generated from the date: the same seed produces the same numbers for everybody, so two people playing on the same day get the same puzzles. Finish the set and your daily streak goes up by one; miss a day and it resets to one.

Rules and scoring

  • Five sequences per day, one attempt each, all with whole-number answers.
  • Answers may be negative only in the subtraction set, and even there the sequence stays above zero.
  • Your best day is the highest number of correct answers out of five, kept under mg-pattern-best.
  • The daily streak is stored under mg-pattern-streak and counts consecutive days you finished the set.
  • Restarting replays today’s five puzzles; it does not generate new ones.

Finding the rule

Start with the gaps between consecutive terms. If they are all the same, the sequence is arithmetic and the next term is the last one plus that gap. If the gaps themselves form a pattern — growing by a constant amount, or alternating between two values — you are looking at a second-order rule, and the answer is one step further out. If the terms grow very fast, test ratios instead: a constant ratio means a geometric sequence, and a ratio that doubles along with the terms means something closer to squaring.

Two of the sets here are recognisable by shape rather than by arithmetic. Perfect squares run 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, so their gaps are the odd numbers 3, 5, 7, 9; once you spot odd gaps increasing by two, the next term is simply the next square. The doubling set, where each term is twice the last plus a small constant, produces gaps that themselves double, which is the giveaway. None of the rules here is arbitrary: every sequence is generated by one of six named rules, and the explanation under each answer names the one that was used.
