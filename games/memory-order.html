How to Play

A group of shapes is shown in a numbered row: left to right is the order you must remember. After a few seconds the numbers vanish and the shapes are shuffled into new positions. Click them in the original order. Each correct click moves the progress bar along; a wrong one ends the round and reveals the order you were meant to reproduce.

Every cleared round adds one more shape, from four up to all eight in the set. The reading time grows with the count, but not by much, because the point of the game is to build a sequence rather than to stare at a picture.

Rules and scoring

  • Four shapes to start; one more per cleared round, up to eight.
  • Only the order matters. Positions on screen are deliberately scrambled after the reveal.
  • Rounds cleared and your longest run are kept in this browser under mg-orderrecall-best and mg-orderrecall-streak.
  • Shapes are also named in their accessible labels, so screen readers can take part.
  • Nothing is timed beyond the reveal itself, and nothing leaves the browser.

Order is a different kind of memory

Recognising which shapes were present is the easy half; the difficulty is that order carries no shape of its own. A useful trick is to turn the sequence into a sentence or a movement: the star comes before the cross, the cross before the circle. Narrating the chain gives your memory a structure to hold on to, which is exactly what the scattered layout takes away.

If a sequence is being awkward, chunk it into two or three groups instead of one long line. Five shapes remembered as "a pair and a triple" are easier than five remembered as five, and the same recoding that helps with digits works here. It also helps to say the names out loud as you click them: hearing the order is a second copy of the information, and two copies survive the shuffle better than one.
