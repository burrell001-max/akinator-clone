How to play

Some squares on the sixteen-cell grid light up in gold for roughly a second. When they go dark again, click each square that was lit. Get them all and the next round lights one more square. Click a square that was dark and the round ends, with the pattern shown in a paler gold so you can see where you went wrong.

Your score is the number of levels cleared, and your best ever is kept in this browser. Starting again begins at three squares: the difficulty curve is the same every time, so the only variable is how well you hold a pattern in mind.

Rules and scoring

  • Level one lights three squares; each cleared level adds one more.
  • Each square can only be clicked once per round, and a wrong click ends the round immediately.
  • Order does not matter — only the set of squares that were lit.
  • Levels cleared and your record are stored under mg-gridflash-best.
  • Nothing is timed, so take as long as you like between clicks.

Remembering a pattern

The fastest way to hold a scattered set of squares is to give it a shape rather than a list. Two squares in the top-left corner and one in the bottom-right is a triangle; three in a diagonal is a line. People who reach double-figure levels almost always describe the pattern to themselves in words like that, because a phrase survives the second of silence better than a set of coordinates.

Chunking works the same way. Instead of counting squares, group them by row or by quadrant: "two in the top row, one in the middle" is one chunk rather than three items. The grid is four by four, which means a row number and a column number is enough to name any cell, and naming cells aloud as they light is a reliable trick for the first six or seven levels. Beyond that, the limit is less about memory span and more about how quickly you can turn an image into a description.
