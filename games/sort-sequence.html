How to play

The set on the page is shuffled. You do not drag anything around: you click the item that belongs next in the order. In the numbers set that means the smallest value remaining, then the next smallest; in the words set it means the word that comes first alphabetically. Correct picks move into the strip along the bottom, and the round ends when the strip is full.

The clock starts on your first click and stops when the last item lands, so thinking time before you begin is free. Mistakes are counted but never end the round — click the right item and carry on.

Rules and scoring

  • Numbers are whole numbers between 1 and 99, all different. Sort ascending.
  • Words are ordinary English words, all different. Sort alphabetically, ignoring case.
  • A click that is not the next item in the order counts as a mistake and stays in the pool.
  • Your score is the time to finish. The best time for each set and length is kept in this browser under mg-sort-best.
  • Changing the set or the length starts a new round immediately.

Getting faster

Sorting a handful of items by hand is really a scanning problem: you are looking for the minimum, again and again. The slow way is to re-read the whole row for every pick. The fast way is to sweep once, note the smallest item, click it, then sweep again from where you left off — your eye keeps the running order instead of restarting.

With numbers, chunking helps more than anything else. Read 34, 7, 61, 19 as "thirties, single digit, sixties, teens" and the order almost announces itself; comparing two-digit numbers digit by digit is slower than classifying them by decade first. With words, compare the first letters only until you have to go deeper, and remember that long words are usually decided by their opening two letters.

Why sorting is a game

Ordering is the most practised operation in all of computing — comparison sorts are the first algorithm most people meet — and yet doing it in your head under time pressure uses a different faculty from writing it in code. There is no trick to memorise and no lucky shuffle: the only thing that improves is how quickly you can hold a partial order in mind.
