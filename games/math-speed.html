How to play

Choose a difficulty and the round starts: sixty seconds on the clock, one question at a time. Type the answer and press Enter, or tap Answer. A correct answer scores a point and moves on instantly; a wrong one is counted, breaks your streak and shows you the right value for a moment before the next question.

Only whole numbers are ever the answer. Division questions are built from an exact product, so nothing lands on a fraction, and subtraction never goes below zero.

Rules and scoring

  • Easy: addition and subtraction with numbers up to 20.
  • Medium: multiplication from 2 to 12, exact division, and small addition or subtraction to 30.
  • Hard: multiplication to 20, exact division, and addition or subtraction with numbers up to 100.
  • One point per correct answer, no bonus for speed within the round — the clock is the pressure.
  • Your best score for each difficulty is kept in this browser under mg-math-best.
  • When the timer reaches zero the round ends and the input locks until you start again.

Scoring more in a minute

Most people lose time in the same three places. The first is reading: a question like 7 × 8 is instant, but 13 × 12 invites you to reach for a written method. Learn the two-digit products up to 20 × 20 as facts rather than sums and the hard level stops being a change of gear.

The second is subtraction. 63 − 28 is faster as 63 − 30 + 2 than as a borrow: round the number you are taking away and put back the difference. The third is division. Every division question here is the reverse of a multiplication, so read 84 ÷ 7 as "seven times what makes eighty-four" and let the times table do the work.

Finally, do not slow down to protect a streak — a wrong answer costs you about half a second and one point, while hesitation costs a second on every question after it. Steady beats careful in a sixty-second round.

Why speed arithmetic is worth practising

Mental arithmetic is not really about numbers; it is about holding a small amount of state in working memory while doing something else. That is the same load a crossword clue or a codebreaking guess puts on you, which is why the games here share a shelf. A minute a day is enough to keep the facts sharp without turning it into homework.
