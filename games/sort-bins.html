How to play

A number appears in the middle of the board and three bins sit underneath it. Each bin is labelled with a rule. Decide which rule the number obeys, then click that bin — or press 1, 2 or 3. A correct sort scores a point and a new number appears immediately. A wrong sort costs a point, resets your streak, and highlights the bin the number actually belonged to before moving on.

You have forty-five seconds. The rules come in four sets and one is chosen at random each round: even and odd with the odd numbers split by divisibility, multiples of five and three, primes and squares, and divisibility by four and three. The bin labels always tell you exactly which rule is in force, so read them before your first click.

Rules and scoring

  • Numbers run from 10 to 99, and every number belongs to exactly one bin.
  • One point per correct sort; one point lost per mistake, never below zero.
  • Your longest correct run is shown as the streak; your best score is kept in this browser under mg-bins-best.
  • The round ends when the timer reaches zero, and the bins lock until you start again.
  • Nothing is fetched and no round is uploaded; the score lives in localStorage only.

Reading the rules quickly

The hard part is not the arithmetic, it is holding the current rule set in mind while the numbers change. Say the rules out loud in order — "five, three, neither" — and test them in that order. A number like 45 fails the first test but passes the second, while 47 fails both and goes straight to the third bin. Because the bins are exclusive and ordered, you never need to check a rule after one has already matched.

Two specific traps are worth learning. In the primes-and-squares round, 49 and 64 are squares but not primes, and 1 is not generated at all; in the divisibility round, 12 is divisible by both four and three, and the four bin takes precedence because it is checked first. The labels in this game spell that precedence out, which is why reading them costs less time than guessing.
