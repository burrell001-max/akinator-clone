How to play

A string of digits appears in gold for a moment, then turns into dots. Type the digits back in the same order and press Enter. Get it right and the next string is one digit longer; get it wrong and the round ends, with the correct sequence shown next to what you typed.

The display time grows with the string, but never as fast as the string does, so the game gets harder in two ways at once. Three digits to begin with is easy; eight or nine is where most people stop.

Rules and scoring

  • Correct rounds increase the length by one digit; a mistake ends the session.
  • Only digits are compared, so spaces, commas and dashes in what you type are stripped first.
  • Your longest successful string is kept in this browser under mg-digitecho-best.
  • Your longest run of correct rounds in one session is kept under mg-digitecho-streak.
  • Nothing is timed and nothing is uploaded.

Holding a number in mind

Digit span is one of the oldest measurements in psychology, and the average adult holds about seven digits without help — which is why phone numbers were once seven long. The trick to doing better is not a bigger short-term store, it is recoding: eight digits become four pairs, or a date, or a rhythm. 4 9 1 5 2 3 6 0 is eight items one way and four two-digit numbers the other.

Say the digits in a rhythm as they appear. Grouping by three has a natural cadence in English, the same one used for reading long numbers aloud, and the cadence survives the blank screen. Long strings also reward the oldest trick of all: repeat them immediately, silently, while the dots are still on screen. Rehearsal buys a couple of seconds, and a couple of seconds is often one whole level.
