How to Play

Each round deals six events from history, technology and exploration. Click them one at a time starting with the earliest. A click is accepted only if the event happened after the last one you placed, so the choices narrow as the list shrinks. When all six are placed, the next round deals a fresh set.

Years are deliberately hidden until an event has been placed — that is where the game is. After the third round the year of every event you saw is listed in order, so a round you guessed through still teaches you the dates.

Rules and scoring

  • Three rounds, six events each, and no event repeats within a session.
  • A wrong click costs nothing but a mistake and a shake; the event stays on the board.
  • Your fewest total mistakes across a completed session is kept in this browser under mg-timeline-best.
  • The timer runs from your first click to the last placement of round three.
  • Start over deals new events and clears the clock, but keeps your record.

Ways to think about a date

Most of these events belong to a cluster you already know, even if the exact year does not come to mind. The first thing to place is the century: the Magna Carta is medieval, the Declaration of Independence is late eighteenth century, the Moon landing is mid twentieth. Sorting by century alone usually resolves the first two or three clicks, because the deal mixes distant eras far more often than it mixes neighbours.

When two events sit close together, look for a causal link rather than a number. The First World War ends before the Second begins and both bracket the Great Depression; Sputnik flies before the first Moon landing; the Berlin Wall falls two years before the web goes public. A chain of consequences is easier to hold in memory than a list of years, and the wrong clicks in this game are almost always two events from the same decade rather than two from different centuries.
