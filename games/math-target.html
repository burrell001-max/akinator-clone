How to play

The row shows four numbers with three gaps between them, followed by an equals sign and a target. Click a gap to select it, then choose an operator from the buttons underneath: plus, minus, times or divide. When all three gaps are filled the equation is checked automatically. If the result matches the target, the puzzle is solved and a new one is dealt; if not, the slots clear and you try again.

The numbers never move. Their order is fixed, and the expression is evaluated strictly left to right — the same way a calculator without brackets works. That rule matters: in 6 + 2 × 3 the answer here is 24, not 12, because the addition happens first. Every puzzle is generated from an expression that does reach the target under those rules, so a solution always exists.

Rules and scoring

  • Four numbers and three operator slots per puzzle; the numbers stay in place.
  • Division is only legal when it comes out exactly; a gap filled with ÷ that would leave a fraction is rejected with a message rather than scored as wrong.
  • Subtraction may take the running total below zero in this game — the target is always a whole number, and negative intermediates are allowed.
  • Your best run of consecutive solves is kept in this browser under mg-forge-best.
  • Changing the difficulty deals a fresh puzzle and keeps the record.

Working out an operator row

Do not test operators from left to right. Work backwards from the target instead: ask what the last operation must have been. If the target is larger than the numbers suggest, multiplication is doing the work; if the target is small, division is. With 3 8 4 2 = 10 the target is only slightly above the largest single number, which usually means a sum or a subtraction near the end rather than a product.

The second habit is to compute the possible running totals as you go rather than the finished expression. After the first operator you have one number instead of two, and after the second you have another; a puzzle with four numbers has only twenty-four distinct operator rows, so writing down the running total at each stage quickly narrows the search to a handful. When two rows both look plausible, prefer the one that keeps every intermediate value a whole number — that is a strong signal that you are on the intended route, since the generator builds puzzles that way.
