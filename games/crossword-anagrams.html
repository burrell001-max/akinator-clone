How to play

Each round shows a crossword-style clue, its enumeration in brackets, and the answer’s letters in a random order. Read the clue, rearrange the letters, type the answer and press Enter. A correct answer scores a point and deals the next clue. A skip moves on with no score.

The letters are exactly the answer — no extra letters, no missing ones — so a wrong answer is almost always a wrong reading of the clue rather than a miscount. You have sixty seconds for eight clues.

Rules and scoring

  • Eight clues per run, drawn at random from a bank of twenty-one.
  • One point per correct answer; skips score nothing and do not cost points.
  • Answers are compared in capitals with punctuation removed, so spacing does not matter.
  • Your best score is stored in this browser under mg-clueanagram-best.
  • The run ends when the timer reaches zero or the eight clues are used up.

Unscrambling by structure

Do not read the letters in the order they are printed. Count them first: the enumeration tells you the length, and the letter mix tells you where the answer probably starts. Long answers usually open with a consonant cluster you can recognise — ST, TR, PL — and endings are easier still, because -ING, -TION, -LY and -ER account for a large share of English words of five letters or more.

The clue is doing more work than the letters are. If the clue is “a person who writes music”, the answer has to be a noun for a person, and English nouns for people very often end in -ER, -IST or -OR. Once you have a candidate ending, the remaining letters either fit the front or they do not, and that check takes a second rather than a minute. Working from the ending inwards is faster than building left to right, which is why crossword solvers read enumerations from the last letter forward when they are stuck.
