Word Guess

Six tries, five letters, one hidden word.

How it works

Type a five-letter word and press enter. After each guess the tiles change colour: green means the letter is in the word and in the right place, amber means the letter is in the word but somewhere else, and grey means the word does not contain that letter at all. You have six guesses.

Repeated letters are handled the way you would expect. If the answer contains one "E" and your guess has two, only the first one is coloured — a second E cannot be both present and accounted for.

Strategy that actually helps

Start with a word built from common letters rather than a clever one. Vowels and the letters R, S, T, L and N appear in most English five-letter words, so a first guess like "stare" or "raise" usually produces more information than a thematic word does.

Do not repeat a letter you already know is absent, and do not move a green letter. If a letter came back amber, it belongs in the word but not where you put it — shifting it to a different position is the single most useful move available. When two guesses leave several possibilities, use the third to test letters you have not tried rather than to guess a word: eliminating four letters can be worth more than a fifty-fifty shot.

Where the words come from

The answer is drawn from a fixed list of common five-letter words written into this page. Names, plurals ending in S where the singular is the real word, and obscure dictionary entries are left out, so a guess only fails when the word really is not in the list. Nothing is fetched and nothing is stored; a new word is one button away.
