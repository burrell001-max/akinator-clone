Mini Crossword

A six-by-six word square: six clues, six answers, each reading across and down.

Clues

    Each numbered clue answers a row and, with the same word, its column — that is what makes the grid a word square.

    How to play

    Pick any square and type one letter. The cursor moves on by itself; arrow keys move around the grid and Backspace steps back a square. Because every square is checked in both directions, a wrong letter breaks two answers at once, which is exactly the feedback you want: fill the squares you are confident about and let the crossings carry you through the rest.

    Check letters colours what you have typed — green for a correct letter, red for a wrong one — without revealing the answers. Show a letter fills one empty square with the right letter and counts as a hint, so try the crossing letters first.

    Rules and scoring

    • The grid is six squares by six, with no black squares — every letter belongs to one row and one column.
    • Each of the six clues has one six-letter answer. The same six words are the rows and the columns, in the same order.
    • You finish when all thirty-six squares hold the correct letters. The timer stops at that moment.
    • Your fastest completion is kept in this browser under mg-word-square-best. Using hints does not block a best time, but the status line tells you how many hints you used.
    • There is no penalty for a wrong letter — check as often as you like.

    Strategy for a word square

    Start with the clue whose answer you are most sure of and type it across its row. In an ordinary crossword that gives you six letters; here it also fills the first letter of six columns, so the whole board immediately narrows. Then read the columns: if a column already begins with a letter you know, the remaining five letters have to make one of the other five answers, and the clue list tells you which ones are still available.

    The useful trick is to count letters. If the word you want for a column is not one of the answers you have left, then the row you typed it from is wrong, even if it looked plausible. Word squares punish guesses that fit the clue but clash with the crossings, which is why they are a better test of spelling than of inspiration.

    Why a word square

    A six-by-six grid with no black squares only works as a crossword when the rows and the columns are all words — that is the definition of a word square, and it is the oldest crossword form there is: examples scratched into walls in Pompeii have the same shape. The constraint is brutal for a puzzle setter and generous for a solver: every letter you place is checked twice.
