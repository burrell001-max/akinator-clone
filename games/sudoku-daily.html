How to Play

Fill the grid so that every row, every column and each of the nine three-by-three boxes contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once. The gold digits are givens and cannot be changed. Click a cell and use the number pad underneath, or use the arrow keys to move around the grid and 1 to 9 to fill the selected cell. Backspace clears an entry.

The grid is generated from today’s date, so everybody who plays today gets the same puzzle, and the generator only removes a digit when the puzzle still has exactly one solution. That means guessing is never necessary: there is always a logical step available.

Rules and scoring

  • Every grid has a unique solution; the Check button never has to report more than one possible answer.
  • Duplicate digits in a row, column or box are marked in red as soon as they appear.
  • Check counts the digits that are wrong and adds them to your mistake count; it never reveals correct answers.
  • Completing today’s grid before midnight keeps your daily streak, stored under mg-sudoku-daily-streak.
  • Your fastest solve is kept under mg-sudoku-daily-best.

Where to look first

Start with the digit that appears most often among the givens. If there are already five sevens on the board, the boxes that still lack a seven usually have only one or two free cells, and one of those cells is often ruled out by a seven elsewhere in its row. Working digit by digit is faster than scanning box by box, because each pass teaches you something about the whole grid.

When singles run out, switch to pairs. Two cells in a box that can only hold the same two digits form a pair: one of them must be the first digit and the other must be the second, so nothing else in that box can use either digit. The same reasoning applies along rows and columns. Most of the middle of a daily grid is solved with pairs and with “this digit can only go here” placements, and both are easier to spot if you note candidates lightly rather than committing early.
