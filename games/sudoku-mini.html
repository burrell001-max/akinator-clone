How to Play

Each row, each column and each two-by-two box must contain 1, 2, 3 and 4 exactly once. Gold digits are givens. Click a cell and press a number on the pad, or arrow around the board and type. Backspace clears the selected cell.

A four-by-four grid is small enough to finish in under a minute, and every puzzle here is generated rather than copied from a book, so replaying gives you new boards instead of the same sixteen cells in a new order.

Rules and scoring

The smallest useful Sudoku

With only four digits, most cells can be found by counting: if a row already has 1, 3 and 4, the empty cell is 2. That is not a simplification for beginners, it is the same logic used on a nine-by-nine grid, just with less to hold in your head. The habit worth taking away is scanning the digit rather than the cell — pick the digit you have seen most often and see where it must go.

Mini grids are also a good place to practise notation. When a cell has two candidates, note them instead of guessing. If two cells in the same row hold the same two candidates, those candidates cannot appear anywhere else in that row, which is the pair rule in its simplest form. Finish a few of these in under a minute and the rule becomes automatic, which is exactly what makes the larger grids manageable.
