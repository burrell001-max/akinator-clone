How to play

Seven numbers are laid out in a random order. Click a tile to lift it, then click a second tile to swap the two. The move counter goes up by one. When the row reads smallest to largest from left to right, the puzzle is solved and the timer stops.

Nothing else changes the numbers: there is no shuffling button to rescue you mid-puzzle and no hints, only the out-of-place counter that tells you how many tiles are sitting away from their final positions. The aim is the fewest moves, not the fastest time — although the clock is shown so you can see how long the thinking took.

Rules and scoring

  • Every row contains seven different numbers, so the sorted order is unique.
  • One swap is one move, whether the two tiles were adjacent or at opposite ends.
  • Your fewest moves is kept in this browser under mg-shift-best; the time is recorded alongside it for interest.
  • Undo all restores the original scramble without resetting the move count — the count is a record of effort, not a criticism.
  • The keyboard works throughout: tab to a tile, press Enter to lift it, tab on, press Enter again.

The maths behind a good swap

Any scramble can be solved in at most n − 1 swaps if you treat it as a set of cycles: pick an unsorted position, put the number that belongs there into it, and repeat. That is the guaranteed ceiling, and the panel's out-of-place counter is a rough guide to how close you are. It is not a perfect score, though, because a single swap can fix two positions at once — swapping a pair that are each other's targets costs one move and clears two tiles.

So the efficient habit is to look for two tiles that should trade places before anything else. Lift a number, read where it ought to sit, and check whether the number already in that slot wants the slot you are holding. When the answer is yes, one move does the work of two. Only when no such pair exists is a longer cycle the right move, and then the rule is to place one tile correctly per swap and not worry about the rest.
