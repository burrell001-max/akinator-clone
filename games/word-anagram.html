How to play

Each round shows the letters of a word in the wrong order. Type the word you think they make and press enter. A correct answer is worth 100 points, less 25 if you used the clue, and moves you straight to the next round. Ten rounds make a game.

Skipping costs nothing but the points for that word, so it is better than a wild guess — and every game has the same ten-round ceiling, which makes scores comparable between attempts.

Rules and scoring

  • 100 points for a word solved without help, 75 with the clue revealed.
  • A wrong guess costs nothing but time; keep trying until you find it or skip.
  • The best total across ten rounds is stored in your browser under a single mg- key.
  • Answers are single English words, five to nine letters, with no proper nouns and no plurals of very short words.

Solving anagrams without guessing

Anagrams are easier than they look once you stop reading them left to right. Look for common openings first: most English words of six letters or more contain one of -ing, -tion, -er or -ed, and spotting a suffix turns the problem from "arrange eight letters" into "arrange five".

Then try vowel patterns. Two vowels together suggest words like reason or outlet; four vowels spread apart suggest something like material. If the clue mentions a category — a place, a feeling, a piece of equipment — decide the part of speech before the letters; a noun-shaped answer narrows the endings you should be testing.

Finally, say it out loud. The ear finds sounds the eye misses, which is why the word often arrives the moment you stop staring at the tiles.
