    How to Play

    This grid is a word square: each of its five rows reads as one of the five answers, and each of its five columns reads as the same five answers, because the square is symmetrical. A handful of letters have been changed to something else. Type over a cell to correct it; when every row and every column matches a clue, the case is closed.

    Use Check whenever you like. It marks every cell that disagrees with the solution in red and adds one to your check count, but it never tells you which clue is at fault — that is the detective work.

    Rules and scoring

    • Five-letter answers, five rows, five columns, one valid square.
    • Between three and five cells are corrupted each case; the count is hidden on purpose.
    • Checks are counted so a solved grid has a record of how much help it took.
    • Your fastest solve is kept in this browser under mg-detective-best.
    • New case re-corrupts the same square, so the answers stay learnable while the puzzle stays fresh.

    Working the clues against each other

    The useful thing about a symmetrical square is that every letter does two jobs: it belongs to a row and to a column, and both of those are answers you can check. Start with the row whose clue you are surest of, read it left to right, and then read the same letters down and see which column stops making sense. The corrupt cell is almost always at the point where the two readings disagree.

    Because the square reuses the same five words, a corrupted letter usually breaks two clues at once, and the pattern of damage is a signature. One wrong letter in the middle of a row will leave both its row and its column reading as nonsense, while a wrong letter at a corner damages exactly two entries as well but is easier to miss, because the words at the edges are shorter to scan. Check the middle of the grid first, then the edges, and only then the four corners.
