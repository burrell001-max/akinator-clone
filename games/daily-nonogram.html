Daily Nonogram

Puzzle for today — the same grid for everyone, all day.

Streak: 0 Solved today: no Filled: 0

Tap a cell to fill it, tap again to mark it empty with an ✕, tap a third time to clear it.

How to play

A nonogram gives you numbers instead of a picture. Each number beside a row or above a column is the length of a run of filled cells, in order. Two numbers mean two separate runs with at least one empty cell between them.

On a row labelled 2 1, six cells long, the row contains a block of two filled cells, then at least one gap, then a single filled cell. The gap could be one cell or several, and the runs could start anywhere that fits — that is the puzzle. Use the ✕ mark for cells you have proved are empty; it does not count as filled and it will not block a win.

You win when every row and every column shows exactly the runs its clue asks for. The check is on the clues, not on one stored answer, so any arrangement that satisfies all twelve clues counts as solved.

Solving a small grid

Start with the biggest numbers. A clue of 6 on a six-cell row means every cell is filled; a clue of 5 leaves only two possible positions. Add the runs and the gaps together: if the numbers in a row plus the minimum gaps between them equal the width, the row is fully determined and can be filled in immediately.

Columns and rows constrain each other, so a single deduction is often worth two. Once a cell is filled, the column clue above it loses one from a run, and once a cell is marked empty it splits a column into two shorter problems. On a six-by-six grid the whole puzzle usually falls to five or six deductions.

The daily part

The grid is generated from the calendar date, so it is identical for everyone playing today and different tomorrow. Solve it and the date is saved in your browser; the streak counts consecutive days back from today, and it survives a refresh because it never left your device.

There is no timer and no failure state — a wrong mark is just a mark. Clear the board and start again if you talk yourself into a corner.
