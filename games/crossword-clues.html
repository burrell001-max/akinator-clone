How to Play

A crossword clue appears with its enumeration in brackets: (7) means the answer has seven letters. Type the answer and press Enter. A correct answer scores ten points and deals the next clue; a skip costs nothing but ends the clue without a score; a hint reveals the first letter and takes three points off it.

You have two minutes from pressing Start. The count of solved clues and your score are shown as you go, and the best score is kept in this browser between visits.

Rules and scoring

  • Ten clues per run, all drawn at random from a bank of twenty-six.
  • Ten points per correct answer, three off per hint used on that clue, nothing for a skip.
  • Spelling matters but case does not; answers are compared in capitals with spaces removed.
  • Your best score is stored under mg-clueclues-best.
  • The round ends at zero on the clock, and the answer box locks until you start again.

Reading a clue like a setter

Start with the enumeration, not the definition. A five-letter clue about a French city is only useful once you know how much room you have, and the bracket rules out three of the four cities you might have guessed. Then look at the tense and number of the definition: a clue that says “ships” wants a plural, while one that says “a ship” does not.

The other habit that pays off is checking every word is doing something. In a good crossword clue, nothing is filler: an adjective may be the definition and the noun may be a piece of wordplay. These clues are straight definitions rather than cryptics, so the rule is simpler — the whole sentence describes the answer — but the discipline transfers. When a clue feels impossible, it is usually because you are taking one word literally that the setter meant as a description.
