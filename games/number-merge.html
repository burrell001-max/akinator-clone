Merge Up

A number puzzle: slide the board, merge equal tiles, keep doubling.

Score 0 Best 0 Top tile 2

Arrow keys or WASD also move the tiles.

How to play

Every move slides all tiles as far as they will go in one direction. When two tiles with the same number collide, they merge into a single tile worth twice as much, and that value is added to your score. After each move that changes the board, one new tile appears in a random empty square: a 2 nine times out of ten, a 4 the rest of the time.

The aim is to keep the board from filling up while you climb the doubling ladder — 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048. You do not have to stop at 2048; the board keeps going until no move is possible.

Rules and scoring

  • A move counts only if at least one tile changes position or two tiles merge.
  • Two tiles merge at most once per move, so a line of four matching tiles makes two pairs, not one giant tile.
  • Score is the sum of every tile value created by a merge. Merging a 64 pair adds 128.
  • Your best score is stored in this browser under mg-merge-best and never leaves the device.
  • The round ends when the board is full and no two neighbouring tiles match. New board resets the score.

Strategy that actually helps

The single habit that separates a board that reaches 512 from one that stalls at 64 is keeping your biggest tile in a corner and never moving it out. Pick a corner — bottom-left is the usual choice — and treat the two edge rows that meet there as the place your large numbers live.

From then on, the moves are almost mechanical. Use only two directions for as long as you can: if your big tile sits bottom-left, you mostly press left and down, which pushes every small tile toward the corner where it can merge. Pressing up or right pulls tiles away from the corner and scatters the order you have built.

The other half of the game is keeping one line free. When the board tightens, the row or column nearest the corner should hold the ladder in descending order — 256, 128, 64, 32 — so that a single press collapses several merges at once. If a stray 2 appears in the middle of that ladder, spend a move getting it out before it blocks the chain.

Why it belongs with the number games

There is no arithmetic to do here beyond doubling, and that is the point. Merge puzzles are about space and order: the numbers are labels for how much room a tile has earned. Playing well means reading a two-dimensional board a few moves ahead, which is the same skill the daily nonogram asks for in a quieter form.
