How to play

Five clues make up each day’s set, all of them written in the style of a quick crossword, each with its enumeration in brackets. Type the answer and press Enter. You get one attempt per clue: the game tells you whether you were right, gives the answer, and moves on. At the end your tally is recorded and your daily streak moves up if you finished the set.

The set is generated from the date, so it is the same for everybody playing that day. Restarting replays those same five clues rather than generating new ones, which makes a second attempt a memory test rather than a puzzle.

Rules and scoring

  • Five clues a day, one attempt each, all straight definitions rather than cryptics.
  • Answers are compared in capitals with spaces and punctuation removed.
  • Your best day — the highest number correct out of five — is kept under mg-crossword-daily-best.
  • The daily streak counts consecutive days you finished the set, stored under mg-crossword-daily-streak.
  • Clues span general knowledge, language and science; none of them requires a dictionary.

Getting better at quick clues

Quick crossword clues are almost always definitions with a grammatical nudge. If the clue says “a” or “the”, the answer is a noun; if it says “to”, the answer is a verb; if it says “describing”, an adjective. The enumeration then decides which noun, verb or adjective fits, and that is usually enough to finish a straight clue without any wordplay at all.

The second skill is recognising the register. A clue that sounds like a dictionary definition wants a plain word, while one that sounds slightly odd is often pointing at a common word used in an unusual sense. “A sound reflected back” is not asking for anything technical; it wants ECHO, and the four-letter enumeration confirms it before you have finished the sentence. Reading the length first and the definition second is the habit that separates a fast solver from a slow one.
