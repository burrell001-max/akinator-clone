Contact MindGenie AI

Last updated: November 27, 2026

Kent B Williams, who runs the site, reads everything sent to this address. Bug reports, wrong guesses, character suggestions, advertising and privacy questions all go to the same inbox, and all of them get read.

Email

hello@mindgenieai.xyz

Please use a plain subject line that says what the message is about, for example "wrong guess", "broken page" or "privacy request". Messages are usually answered within three to five working days. There is no support phone number and no live chat, because there is no support team behind them.

Before you write

Three pages answer most questions faster than an email can. The how to play page covers the rules of a round, the FAQ covers what players ask most often, and the blog explains why the engine behaves the way it does — the five answer options, the fourteen-question limit and the scoring behind both.

If the question is about a specific wrong guess, the character's own guide page is the fastest place to look. Each guide names the lookalike that is most likely to steal that round and the answer that separates them. The guides are reachable from the movie, TV, anime, video game and history character lists.

What to include

A wrong guess

Say which character you were thinking of, roughly how many questions the round ran, and which question you felt pushed it off course. If you can remember the answer you gave that felt wrong, that is the most useful part. You do not need to send your session ID; if you still have the result screen open, the character names are enough to investigate.

A broken page or a bug

Include the address of the page, what you were doing, what you expected, and what actually happened. The browser and device help more than you would expect: "Safari on an iPhone 12" or "Chrome on Windows 11" narrows a problem quickly. A screenshot is welcome as long as it does not contain anything personal.

A character that should be added

Name the character and the work they come from. Characters with distinctive, stable traits are easier to add than characters who change heavily between versions. Sending the same suggestion twice does not move it up any list, but it is not ignored either.

Advertising, consent or privacy

If an ad looks broken or inappropriate, include the page address and, if possible, the advertiser's name or the ad's destination. For a question about what is stored when you play, read the privacy policy first; it explains what the game keeps, what Google's advertising services may set, and how to ask for a record to be removed. If you have already read it and something is still unclear, write to the address above and say which section you are asking about.

Corrections to a guide or an article

The guides and the longer articles quote real numbers from the engine: the distance between two characters across the twenty-eight traits, the five answer weights, the six-question floor, the 0.35 margin and the fourteen-question cap. Those figures are read from the code that runs the site rather than estimated, so if one of them is wrong it is worth fixing.

When you report a correction, name the page and quote the sentence that is wrong. "The Shrek guide says his nearest neighbour is SpongeBob at 0.161, but the round guesses Pikachu" is the kind of report that can be checked in a minute. Corrections that are accepted are made on the page itself, and the publish date stays as it was.

Privacy and data requests

The game has no accounts, so there is no profile to look up and nothing tied to your name. The privacy policy sets out what the site counts, what Google's advertising services may set, and what is kept when you play. Read that first if the question is about cookies or consent; it is written to be read rather than to be skipped.

If you want a message you sent to be deleted, say so in a reply to the same thread and it will be removed once the question it raised has been answered. You do not need to send identification to make that request, and you should not send it anyway.

Teachers, classrooms and research

The game is free to use in a classroom, a library or a club, and there is nothing to sign up for. The guide to playing with a group sets out a turn-taking format and a scoring system that work with a class, and it can be printed or read aloud.

If you are writing about the site, the numbers worth quoting are these: fifty characters, twenty-eight traits, five answer options, a fourteen-question ceiling and a 0.35 margin between the leader and the runner-up. Attribution to MindGenie AI is enough; there is no press kit and no need to ask permission first.

Accessibility

The site has had a readability pass, and the text on the guide and article pages is deliberately dark on a light background rather than the other way round. That does not mean it is right everywhere. If a page is hard to read on your screen, if the controls are awkward with a keyboard, or if something is announced badly by a screen reader, say which page and which browser or device you are using. Specific reports get fixed; "the site is hard to use" cannot be acted on.

What this address cannot help with

  • Password resets. There are no accounts, so there is nothing to reset.
  • Ad billing or account issues. Those belong to Google, not to this site; the advertising settings are in your Google account.
  • Guaranteed character additions. Suggestions are read, but the roster changes on its own schedule.
  • Legal or medical advice. A guessing game is a poor authority on either.

What not to send

Please do not send passwords, identity documents, payment details or anything else sensitive. The game has no accounts, so there is nothing to recover and no password to reset. Messages that contain personal data are deleted once the question is answered.

Please also do not send screenshots of advertisements containing personal information — an account name in a corner, a delivery address, a notification banner. The page address and the advertiser's name are enough to investigate an ad, and they are the parts that can actually be acted on.
