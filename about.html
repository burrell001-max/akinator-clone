About MindGenie AI

Last updated: November 27, 2026

MindGenie AI is a free character guessing game that runs in a browser. You think of a character, the Genie asks a short run of yes-or-no style questions, and it ends with a name. There is no account, no download and no payment. This page explains why the site exists, who runs it, how the guesses are actually produced, and what happens to the information you type or tap while you play.

Why the site exists

The twenty-questions format is old and simple: one person holds a secret, the other asks questions that split the possibilities until only one answer is left. It works well without a second person, which is exactly the kind of game a browser can host. Most versions of it, though, want an account, push an app install, or take a while to load on a phone. This one was built in the opposite direction: one page, one roster of characters, and a round that starts as soon as the page is ready.

What it does

The Genie carries a roster of characters drawn from films, television, anime, video games and history. Every character in that roster has recorded values for a fixed set of traits: whether they are fictional, whether they have powers, whether they wear a mask, lead a group, use a weapon, and so on. A round is the process of narrowing that roster down using your answers until one character is far enough ahead of the rest.

The site also publishes written guides. Each category page lists the characters in that category and how difficult each one tends to be, and each character guide explains the traits that identify that character and the questions players most often answer inconsistently.

Who runs it

MindGenie AI is written and maintained by Kent B Williams, an independent developer who works on it in their own time. There is no company behind it and no editorial team. That means the site is small and the person answering your email is also the person who fixes the bug. If a guess is wrong, a page is broken, or a character is missing, the contact page reaches them directly at hello@mindgenieai.xyz.

How the guesses are produced

Nothing about a guess is a coincidence, and nothing is looked up about you. The engine scores every character in the roster against the answers you have given so far. Characters that fit your answers closely get more weight, characters that contradict them get less. To choose the next question, it tests each unasked trait and measures how unevenly that trait is spread across the characters still in contention. The trait that would split the field most sharply is the one you are asked about next.

The round does not stop on the first plausible name. It runs for at least six questions and never more than fourteen, and it ends early only when the leading character is clearly ahead of the next one. If your answers are contradictory, no character pulls ahead, the round runs longer, and the final guess is more likely to be wrong. That is why the middle answers exist: "probably" and "not sure" carry less weight than a confident yes or no, so a half-remembered detail does less damage than a guess dressed up as certainty.

After the guess you can say whether it was right. If it was wrong, you can optionally type the name you had in mind. That correction is stored without any name, email address or account attached, and it is used to see which characters the engine cannot yet tell apart.

What stays on your device

The site keeps a small amount of information in your browser's local storage so the game behaves sensibly between visits. That covers your chosen interface language, a short history of your last rounds, and a local count of page views used as a fallback for the visitor counter. Those values stay in your browser and are not sent to us as a profile of you. The site itself does not set advertising or tracking cookies of its own; the cookies you may receive come from Google's advertising and analytics services. The privacy policy lists them one by one, and the cookie policy explains how to remove them.

How the site is funded

MindGenie AI is free, and advertising through Google AdSense is what covers the hosting. Ads appear in fixed positions on some pages. They have no influence on the roster, the questions or the guess: the engine does not read ad data, and nothing about an advertiser changes which character wins a round. Visitors in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland are asked for consent through Google's consent message before personalised advertising cookies are used.

How the content is kept up to date

The guides are written from the same trait data the engine uses, so when a character's values change or a new character joins the roster, the matching guide is revised rather than left to drift. Corrections from players are the main source of those changes: a character that is repeatedly missed is usually a character whose traits are too close to a neighbour's. Pages carry a last-updated date when their substance changes.
