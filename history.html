How to pick and answer for this category

The biggest difference with historical figures is the first question, "Is your character completely fictional?", which is a clear No for everyone on this page. After that, the trickiest questions are about morals and role. Whether Genghis Khan counts as a villain, or whether Cleopatra counts as a hero, depends on whom you ask, and honest in-between answers are the right choice.

History also blurs fact and legend. Details of Joan of Arc's visions, Tesla's rivalries and Cleopatra's life were shaped by later retellings, and the Genie answers about the popular picture of each person. If a question about weapons, magic or special abilities comes up, think about how the person is usually portrayed, then use "Probably" for anything that would require a debate.

Why there are five historical figures and not fifty

A real person is harder to record than a fictional one. Characters in stories have a fixed set of facts their writers control; historical figures have a life, a reputation, and often a century of argument about both. Adding a ruler or a scientist to the roster means deciding which widely accepted version of them the questions will describe, and that takes longer than adding a character from a film with one clear appearance.

The five here were chosen because their popular image is stable enough to answer questions about. Where a question depends on interpretation — whether a conqueror counts as a villain, whether a ruler counts as a hero — the honest answer is usually a soft one, and the guides say so on each page.

How the difficulty rating is worked out

Each character's rating comes from its distance to the nearest other character in the engine's trait data. A figure who differs from everyone else on several answers is easy to separate and lands in the easy group. A figure whose nearest neighbour differs by only one or two answers lands in the hard group, because a single uncertain reply is enough to move the round to the wrong person. The rating describes the data, not the fame of the person.
