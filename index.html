      Frequently Asked Questions

      What is an AI guessing game?

      It is a game where a program asks you questions to work out a character you are thinking of. Here the program is the Genie, and a round takes a handful of questions.

      Is MindGenie AI free to play?

      It is. No payment, no download and no account are involved at any point.

      How accurate is MindGenie AI?

      It depends on the pick and on your answers. Recognisable characters usually resolve quickly; a character who closely resembles another one often does not.

      Can I play 20 questions online with MindGenie AI?

      That is exactly the format: you hold the secret, the Genie asks, and the round ends when one name is clearly ahead.

      How many questions does the Genie ask?

      A round runs between six and fourteen questions, depending on how quickly your answers separate one character from the rest.

      Can I play MindGenie AI on my phone?

      Yes. The whole game is a web page, so a phone browser is enough and nothing needs installing.

      What kinds of characters can I think of?

      Film, television, anime, video game and historical picks all appear in the roster, and any other character is still worth trying.

      What happens if the Genie guesses wrong?

      You can mark the guess as wrong. That rating is stored without anything identifying you, and it is used to see which characters are being confused.

      Do I need to create an account?

      No. Nothing is created, stored or verified, and there is no profile to manage.

      Is this a genie guesser or mind reader game?

      It is a guessing game with a genie as the questioner. It is independent, and it is not connected to Akinator or any other guessing site.

      About MindGenie, the Free AI Character Guessing Game

      MindGenie AI is a free browser-based AI guessing game and mind reader inspired by classic "20 questions" style games. Think of any character — real, fictional, historical, or from pop culture — and the Genie will ask you a short series of simple yes-or-no style questions to work out who you're thinking of.

      Under the hood, each answer narrows down a large pool of possible characters by comparing the traits you've confirmed or ruled out against traits already known about thousands of characters. The more distinctive a character is — a unique power, a rare job, a specific era — the fewer questions it usually takes. Characters who share a lot of traits with someone more famous, like a supporting cast member or a minor villain, tend to take longer and occasionally stump the Genie entirely.

      When you rate a guess as right or wrong, that rating is saved as an anonymous statistic. We review these stats to see which characters are guessed easily and which stump the Genie, and use them to decide which characters and questions to add or improve in future updates.

      No account, download, or payment is required — just open the page and start playing. Read more about how the Genie works →

      How to Play the Character Guessing Game
      1. Think of any character — a person, fictional hero, villain, celebrity, or historical figure — and hold them firmly in mind before you start.
      2. Click "Start Playing" to begin a new round.
      3. Answer each question with Yes, No, Probably, Probably not, or I'm not sure, based only on what's actually true of the character you're thinking of.
      4. After a series of questions — usually 6 to 14 — the Genie reveals who it thinks you were picturing.
      5. Rate whether the guess was correct, then play again with a harder or more obscure pick.

      A couple of tips: answer honestly rather than trying to "trick" the Genie — inconsistent answers usually just slow down the guess rather than beat it. And if you get stuck on the same character every round, try switching categories below for a fresh challenge.

      How the Genie Narrows Fifty Characters Down to One

      There is no fixed script of questions and no lookup table of answers. Every character in the roster carries the same twenty-eight traits — fictional or real, human or not, powered, masked, armed, royal, animated, academic — and every answer you give is stored as a number between zero and one. The engine scores all fifty characters against those numbers, asks whichever unasked question best separates the current leaders, and repeats.

      The five answer buttons carry their own weights rather than counting as yes or no: Yes is 1.0, Probably is 0.75, Not sure is 0.5, Probably not is 0.25 and No is 0.0. A partial answer never removes a character from contention; it nudges their score. That is why a hesitant round stays open longer than a confident one, and why “not sure” is a position rather than a skip.

      Three rules decide when the asking stops: at least six questions have been asked, the leader is 0.35 ahead of the runner-up, or fourteen questions are used up. On a roster this size the fourteen-question ceiling ends most rounds, because characters who answer alike stay within a whisker of each other for a long time. That is not the Genie being slow — it is the cost of identifying one of fifty people with twenty-eight broad questions instead of one of a million with twenty perfect ones.
