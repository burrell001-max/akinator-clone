Thor: A Character Guide for MindGenie AI

By Kent B Williams · Published November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Thor answers yes to power, heroism, royalty, a weapon, a team, fame, a transformation and an alien origin. That is a lot of strong splits at once, and the only thing that slows his round is the partial on the human question.

Quick facts

First appearance
Journey into Mystery #83 (1962)
Created by
Stan Lee, Larry Lieber and Jack Kirby
Role
Asgardian god of thunder, king of Asgard
Known for
Mjolnir, lightning and a very long family feud
Powers
Superhuman strength, weather control, long life

Why the round is short

The alien-or-space question is the strongest single split he has, because the roster holds very few characters from another world. Combined with royalty and a weapon, it leaves almost nothing standing.

The human question is stored as a partial because he looks human and lives among humans, which is also what keeps him close to Wonder Woman on the sheet.

Who he is

The son of Odin, raised believing he would inherit Asgard, banished to Earth to learn humility, and eventually king. His story is a family argument conducted with hammers across nine realms.

He is stored as a hero despite the films' habit of making him the cause of his own problems, because the engine records role rather than quality.

Where players get tripped up

The weapon question. In the films Mjolnir is destroyed and he fights without it, so players answer no, but the engine stores a yes.

The team question is a yes: Avengers, and before that Asgard's army. A no sends the round towards lone gods and villains.

Why the Genie might guess Wonder Woman instead

Closest match on the sheet: Wonder Woman, at an average distance of 0.16 across 28 questions.

They differ on 7 questions: costume or disguise no against partly; alien or space yes against no; famous companion partly against no; mind over strength no against partly; major transformation yes against no; paying job no against partly; real name known yes against partly. Answer those differences deliberately and the round resolves; answer by habit and the guess is a coin toss between the two.

Comics, myth and the films

The Marvel Thor borrows names and relationships from Norse myth and almost nothing else: the comic strips out the fatalism and adds a superhero team. Players who know the myths answer differently about death, prophecy and Loki.

The films then changed the character again, making him comedic and, briefly, unworthy of the hammer. The stored answers follow the film version, which is what most players picture.

Frequently asked questions

Is Thor human?

He is an Asgardian, not a human, but he looks and lives like one, so the stored answer is a partial.

Why is he confused with Wonder Woman?

Both are superhuman royals with weapons and teams, and both are stored as only partly human.

Does Thor have powers?

Yes. Strength, weather and long life are stored as a clean yes.
