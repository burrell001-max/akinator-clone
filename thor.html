Thor: Fourteen Firm Yeses and a Neighbour at 0.161

By Kent B Williams · Updated November 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Thor is the most agreeable character in the movie pool: fourteen firm yeses, three halves and eleven nos. Almost every question a player expects to answer yes, he answers yes to — hero, powers, space, weapon, royalty, team, nemesis, transformation, fantasy, famous, named, protagonist. That is exactly why he is filed under medium rather than easy. A character who says yes to everything sits close to every other character who says yes to everything, and the roster has several.

The sheet, read as a list

Thor's yeses: fictional, powered, hero, from outer space, signature weapon, part of a team, known to almost everyone on Earth, royalty, nemesis, transformation, fantasy setting, speaks, public name, sole protagonist. His halves: human, animated, famous sidekick. His nos: underwater, mask, science, villain, book-primary, mind over muscle, school, mentor, romance, sea voyage, paying job.

Two of those nos are worth pausing on, because they are the answers that stop Thor from being indistinguishable from everyone else. Science is a no even though the films are full of astrophysicists: the sheet is describing Thor, not Jane Foster, and Thor's competence is strength and lightning rather than enquiry. Mind over muscle is a no for the same reason, and it is the answer that splits him from Wonder Woman.

Wonder Woman at 0.161: seven traits, one of them decisive

Wonder Woman is Thor's nearest neighbour across the whole movie pool, at an average distance of 0.161. They agree on twenty-one of twenty-eight. The seven that differ:

The first row is the one to remember. Thor does not wear a mask or a disguise; he turns up in person and announces himself. Wonder Woman wears armour that the sheet treats as costume-adjacent, hence the half. If you answer "yes, he has a costume" because a red cape counts, you push the round straight at her.

The second and fifth rows are the ones the engine leans on hardest. Space and transformation are both firm for Thor, both negative for Wonder Woman, and both broad questions that the engine asks early.

Goku and Kratos at 0.179: two different kinds of neighbour

Second place is a tie. Goku differs on seven traits and Kratos on seven, but the splits are not the same shape, which is useful when a round goes wrong.

Against Goku the splits are animation, weapon, team, sidekick, royalty, fantasy setting and sole protagonist. Thor is the royal one, the team member, the named protagonist and the weapon user; Goku is the animated one with a sidekick and a half-claimed fantasy world. A wrong answer on animation is what most often produces Goku.

Against Kratos the splits are hero, space, villainy, animation, team, fame and royalty. Thor is a firm hero, a firm space character, a firm team member and a firm royal; Kratos is a half-hero with a half-villain history who belongs to no team and no throne. Here the damage comes from denying the team or the royalty, not from animation.

So the two neighbours fail for opposite reasons. That is the practical value of knowing both: a wrong guess of Goku means you probably answered no to something animated; a wrong guess of Kratos means you probably answered no to something institutional.

The Loki trap

The nemesis trait is a firm yes, and some players answer no on the grounds that Loki is a brother rather than an enemy. The sheet treats the adversarial relationship as the thing being asked about, regardless of the family tree, and the films spend four of them making that point explicitly. Answer yes.

The sidekick trait is a half, which is a fair reflection of a character who works with a rotating cast — Warriors Three, Lady Sif, Valkyrie, the Hulk — without ever having one devoted companion the way the Doctor has a companion. If you answer a firm yes because Valkyrie fights beside him, you have added 0.5 to the true character's distance for no reason.

Animated is a half, and that is deliberate

The animation question is stored as 0.5 for Thor, which looks like a mistake until you remember how much animated Marvel material exists: the 1966 series, the various direct-to-video features, What If…? and the Lego specials. The sheet is not saying "sometimes animated"; it is saying the trait does not cleanly apply, and a player with only the films in mind should answer the same way. A firm no here is more damaging than it looks, because animation is one of the questions the engine likes to ask early.

Which Thor, and which mythology

There are at least five characters a player might mean. The Marvel comics Thor has a mortal alter ego, Donald Blake, and a long history of losing and reclaiming Mjolnir. The film Thor is a prince learning humility across four solo films and four ensemble ones. The mythological Thor is a red-bearded god of thunder with a hammer and a short temper, and no secret identity at all. The Jane Foster Thor is a different character carrying the name. The God of War Thor is a heavy, bitter antagonist who would answer the hero question very differently.

The sheet describes the mainstream comic-and-film character: royal, heroic, publicly named, spacefaring, transforming. If you are thinking of the antagonist version, or of a specific comic run where Thor is unworthy and powerless, expect the round to drift — and expect the drift to be toward Kratos rather than Wonder Woman.

Where players lose this round

  • Costume. A cape is not a mask. The sheet has Wonder Woman at half and Thor at zero; answering yes is the single most common way to lose him to her.
  • Science. He travels with scientists and is studied by them; he is not associated with the field himself.
  • Humanity. He looks human and is an Asgardian. This is a half, and answering it firmly either way costs you.
  • Romance. The films have a love interest; the sheet stores romance at zero for the character's own story, which is about family and worthiness.
  • Paying job. Prince is not a job, and Avenger is not salaried. Answer no.

Playing Thor deliberately

  1. Say yes to space, royalty, weapon and team in that order if you get the chance. They are the four traits that place him fastest.
  2. Say no to the mask, even though the costume is iconic. It is the answer that separates him from Wonder Woman.
  3. Use halves for human, animated and sidekick rather than guessing firmly.
  4. Say yes to the nemesis. The brother is the enemy for this purpose.
  5. Expect the round to reach fourteen questions. Every character does with consistent answers; the guess settling early is not the same as the round ending early.
