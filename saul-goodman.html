⚖️ Saul Goodman

Saul Goodman is the flashy, fast-talking lawyer from Breaking Bad who gets his own series, Better Call Saul. The name is an alias: the man behind it is Jimmy McGill, a small-time attorney whose rise and fall is the story of the prequel.

Why the Genie sometimes needs a few extra questions

Saul Goodman is a medium pick because the Genie has to separate him from Walter White and other Breaking Bad characters. Questions about a job, a transformation, and a changed identity are the most useful.

Background on Saul Goodman

Saul first appeared in Breaking Bad in 2009, played by Bob Odenkirk. Better Call Saul, created by Vince Gilligan and Peter Gould, aired from 2015 to 2022 and follows Jimmy McGill through his years as a struggling lawyer in Albuquerque, New Mexico. His complicated relationships with his brother Chuck, his partner Kim Wexler, and the fixer Mike Ehrmantraut drive the story.

Across the series, Jimmy gradually adopts the Saul Goodman persona, with its loud suits and billboard-style advertising, and eventually uses other names to hide. The show is often praised for its slow-burn storytelling and is considered one of the best television dramas of its era.

Traits that give them away

  • A lawyer. His profession is central, so the job question is a yes.
  • An alias. Saul Goodman is a persona, which affects the identity question.
  • A transformation. Jimmy McGill's change into Saul is the core of the series.
  • No powers. He relies on talk and cunning.
  • A morally gray figure. He is neither a clear hero nor a clear villain.

Easy to confuse with

Saul Goodman is often confused with Walter White, because they share a universe, and with other TV lawyers such as Harvey Specter or Matt Murdock. The job question helps a lot, since Walter is a teacher. His brother Chuck and his partner Kim are other Better Call Saul characters who share much of the setting.

Tips for playing as Saul Goodman

Decide whether you mean Jimmy McGill or Saul Goodman, since the answers about identity differ. For a harder round, try Mike Ehrmantraut or Kim Wexler.
