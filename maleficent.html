Maleficent: A Villain the Engine Also Calls the Protagonist

By Kent B Williams · Updated November 27, 2026 · 7 min read

Maleficent carries a combination the rest of the movie pool does not: she is stored as a firm villain and as the sole, title-billed protagonist of her own story at the same time. That is not a contradiction in the data — it is an accurate description of a character who was the antagonist of a 1959 animated film and the protagonist of a 2014 live-action one. Everything difficult about a Maleficent round follows from that split, and from the fact that she is filed as easy anyway.

Quick facts

Horned headdress and staff; no mask, no weapon, no armour

The sheet: ten yeses, four halves, fourteen nos

Firm yeses: fictional, powered, villain, known to almost everyone, royalty, nemesis, fantasy setting, speaks, public name, sole protagonist. Halves: human, animated, mind over muscle, transformation. The fourteen nos are the interesting group, because they include hero, underwater, mask, science, space, weapon, team, companion, book-primary, school, mentor, romance, sea voyage and paying job.

Three of those nos do most of the separating:

  • Weapon. The staff is a focus, not an armament. She curses and transforms; she does not fight with an object, which puts her at zero where Thor and Kratos are at one.
  • Team. She works alone, sometimes with a raven. Answering yes because of the fairy court pushes the round toward Thanos, who is a firm team character.
  • Companion. Diaval is loyal and famous-ish, but the sheet stores the trait at zero for her, unlike the Doctor's firm yes.

GLaDOS and Kratos at 0.196: two neighbours, opposite reasons

Her nearest neighbours tie. GLaDOS agrees on twenty of twenty-eight; Kratos agrees on nineteen. They are nothing alike as characters, and the splits show why the engine still finds them close.

Against GLaDOS, the eight differences are humanity, science, animation, fame, mind, transformation, fantasy setting and sole protagonist. Maleficent is a magical fairy in a fairy-tale world who is famous and the lead of her own film; GLaDOS is a science-built AI in a facility who is only half-famous and not a protagonist at all. The trap here is the mind trait: Maleficent is stored at a half, GLaDOS at a firm yes. A player who answers "yes, she is clever and manipulative" hands the round to a computer.

Against Kratos, the nine differences are hero, villain, animation, weapon, fame, companion, royalty, mind and transformation. Maleficent is a firm villain and a firm royal with no weapon; Kratos is a half-hero with a half-villain history, a firm weapon and no throne. Answering "villain" with a half because the 2014 film redeems her is the most common way to lose her to Kratos — and to Thanos, who sits at 0.214 on the same kind of answer.

The redemption problem

This is the heart of a Maleficent round. The 1959 character is an offended fairy who curses a baby and is killed by a prince. The 2014 character is the same fairy rewritten as the wronged party, the protector and the film's emotional centre. Both are canon; both are Maleficent.

The sheet resolves the conflict by keeping the villain answer firm and adding the protagonist answer, which is only possible because the two questions are not opposites. A character can be the villain of one story and the protagonist of another, and the engine stores each assertion separately. So the correct way to answer both is: villain, yes — that is the role she is famous for; sole protagonist, yes — that is her own film.

Players who try to average the two versions into "morally grey" end up answering half on both, which is the one answer the sheet does not contain.

Transformation is only half, and that is right

She becomes a dragon in the 1959 film, and the 2014 film makes the loss and recovery of her wings the centre of its story. Both are changes, neither is a permanent change of self in the way the Doctor's regeneration or Buzz's acceptance of what he is can be described. The sheet sits at 0.5, which is the honest answer for a character whose transformations are roles and shapes rather than identity.

Humanity is a half for the same reason: she is a fairy who appears as a tall woman, and the sheet declines to call her either.

Which Maleficent, and which Sleeping Beauty

Beyond the two films, there is the original Perrault and Grimm fairy tale, where the wicked fairy is an unnamed slighted guest and the christening curse is the whole of her part. There is the Kingdom Hearts version, which is a recurring antagonist across several games. There is the Disney Villains franchise, where she is a brand as much as a character. None of them changes the trait sheet, because none of them changes what she does: magic, a curse, a royal court and a fairy-tale world.

What changes is the answer a player gives to hero, villain and protagonist. Answer those for the 1959 role and the 2014 starring role, in that order, and the rest of the round behaves.

Where players lose this round

  • Answering half on villain. The half belongs to Kratos and Thanos, not to her. She is the villain answer.
  • Answering yes on weapon. The staff is not stored as one. This is the single cleanest split against the armed characters.
  • Answering yes on mind. She is cunning, not an intellect character. The half exists; a firm yes pushes toward GLaDOS.
  • Denying royalty. She is a fairy queen in all but name and the sheet stores the trait as a firm yes, which separates her from nearly every other villain in the roster.
  • Answering yes on team. She has a raven, not an organisation.

Playing Maleficent deliberately

  1. Say yes to villain and yes to magic. Nothing else in the movie pool combines them with royalty.
  2. Say yes to royalty. It is the trait that keeps her away from Thanos and Kratos.
  3. Say no to the weapon and no to the team. Both are traps that feel true.
  4. Use halves for human, animated, mind and transformation rather than guessing.
  5. Expect fourteen questions. The cap is what ends rounds in this roster, not the margin.
