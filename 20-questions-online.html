20 Questions Online: Play Against the AI Genie

20 Questions is one of the oldest guessing games there is: one player thinks of something, and the other asks yes-or-no questions to work out what it is. MindGenie AI turns you into the player with the secret and puts the Genie in the questioner's seat, so you can play 20 Questions online any time without needing a friend.

How this version differs from the classic game

In the classic game the questioner is limited to exactly twenty questions and only gets Yes or No. Here the Genie typically asks somewhere between 6 and 14 questions, and you have a few more answer options: Yes, No, Probably, Probably not and Not sure. Those softer answers matter, because people often aren't certain about a trait, such as whether a character is technically a hero or a villain.

Why the questions get you somewhere

A strong 20 Questions player doesn't ask random things. Each question should remove about half of the remaining possibilities, in the same way a binary search works. The Genie follows the same idea: early questions are broad (real or fictional, human or not), and later ones get specific. Tips if you want to play it with friends

  • Take turns thinking of a character and let each friend predict whether the Genie will get it.
  • Set a rule that nobody can pick a character from the top of their head. Choose someone from a show or game you've watched or played recently.
  • Score it: one point if the Genie guesses correctly, one point for you if it doesn't.

Ideas for tough rounds

Try minor characters, villains who look like heroes, or two characters from the same franchise with similar roles. The 50 characters that stump the Genie list is a good source of challenges. How this compares to the tabletop version

The version most people learn first is played out loud, with a questioner who is allowed to ask anything and a strict limit of twenty questions. This one changes three things. The questioner is a program, so it will not tire or lose track. The limit is soft: a round stops between six and fourteen questions, depending on how quickly the answers separate one character from the rest. And the answers have five options rather than two, which matters when the thing you are thinking of is only half-remembered.

The parts that do not change are the ones that make the game work. You still hold the secret, you still cannot give hints, and a question you answer dishonestly still costs you later in the round.

Reading the last screen

The guess arrives with a prompt asking whether it was right. Answering honestly is the only thing that improves the roster: a wrong guess where you type the name you had in mind tells the engine which two characters it cannot yet tell apart. Nothing else about you is attached to that record, and there is no way for a rating to change the round you have just finished.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as the 20 Questions board game?

No. It is the same guessing idea played online against the Genie, with five answer options instead of two and a round that stops when the engine is confident.

Do I need to install anything?

No. The game runs in the browser, on a phone or a computer, with nothing to download.

Can I play a round with a friend?

Yes. One of you holds the character and the other watches. Agree the answers out loud before you tap them, or the round will drift.

Why did the round end before twenty questions?

Because the engine stops once one character is clearly ahead. If the answers are close between two characters, the round runs longer.
