⚔️ Kratos

By Kent B Williams · Published 1 November 2026

Kratos is a hardened warrior protagonist who began his story fighting gods of Greek mythology before later games shifted his setting to Norse mythology. His arc from vengeful antihero to more reflective father figure is one of gaming's most-cited character transformations.

Why the Genie takes a bit longer with this one

Kratos sits in the medium tier because his setting changed significantly partway through the franchise (Greek to Norse mythology), which means the Genie sometimes needs extra questions about which mythology or time period before narrowing him down from other mythology-inspired warriors.

Background on Kratos

Kratos is the lead character of the God of War series from Santa Monica Studio, which began on the PlayStation 2 in 2005. In the original Greek-mythology games, he is a Spartan warrior who serves the gods, is tricked into killing his own family, and then wages a bloody war against Olympus, including a fight with Ares and later Zeus.

The 2018 reboot moved the series to the world of Norse mythology and presented an older, quieter Kratos raising his young son, Atreus. That game was praised for its storytelling and its single-camera presentation. Kratos's signature weapons include the Blades of Chaos and, later, the Leviathan Axe.

Traits that give them away

  • Mythological setting. Greek and Norse worlds make the fantasy or mythical setting question a yes.
  • Signature weapons. The Blades of Chaos and Leviathan Axe define his combat.
  • A demigod. He is the son of Zeus, so superhuman ability questions are a yes.
  • Anti-hero. He fights for his own reasons, so the hero and villain questions are mixed.
  • A companion. In the Norse games, Atreus and the head of Mimir travel with him.

How a round usually runs

A Kratos round takes a little longer than the biggest names because so much of his profile is generic. He is fictional, human, powered, violent and fictional, and none of those traits is rare on its own. What narrows him down is the combination of a warrior's role with a family story and a setting drawn from myth.

Easy to confuse with

Kratos is usually confused with other grim, powerful warriors from games and myth. Dante from Devil May Cry, Geralt of Rivia from The Witcher, and Thor from the Marvel films and Norse myth all share a similar vibe. The most useful separators are the Spartan background, the Blades of Chaos, and the Greek and Norse gods.

Tips for playing as Kratos

Kratos changed a lot between the early and later games, so decide which one you are thinking of. The earlier Kratos is far angrier and more villainous, while the later one is a protective father. For a harder round, try Atreus, who is also a major figure in the series.

When to pick Kratos

Pick Kratos when you want a round that rewards patience. He is a good choice if you know both eras of his story, because the questions about weapons and setting play out differently depending on which one you have in mind.

Answers that go wrong

The hero question is where players hesitate. He is the protagonist of his own story and a very destructive one, and answering a confident yes or a confident no both cost the round accuracy. Probably is the honest answer.

Frequently asked questions

Does Kratos have powers?

Yes. His strength and durability go well beyond a normal person, and both are recorded as abilities.

Is he human?

He is a human in origin, though his story involves becoming something more. The engine treats him as human.

Why does the Genie ask about weapons?

His weapons are a defining part of him across both eras of the series, so a yes there narrows the field quickly.

Which era should I answer for?

Either, as long as you stay with it. The older and newer games differ in setting, companions and weapons.
