🎩 Napoleon Bonaparte

By Kent B Williams · Published 1 November 2026

Napoleon Bonaparte was a French military leader who rose to power during the French Revolution and later crowned himself Emperor of the French. His military campaigns across Europe and eventual exile make him one of the most-studied figures in Western history.

Why the Genie usually gets this one

A specific era (early 1800s Europe), a rare title (self-crowned emperor), and an extensively documented military career make Napoleon easy to separate from other rulers. The Genie usually only needs to confirm region and time period.

Background on Napoleon Bonaparte

Napoleon Bonaparte was born in 1769 in Ajaccio, on the island of Corsica, and rose through the French army during the French Revolution. He seized power in 1799 and crowned himself Emperor of the French in 1804. His military victories, such as the Battle of Austerlitz in 1805, made him one of history's most celebrated commanders.

His campaigns reshaped Europe, but his invasion of Russia in 1812 was disastrous. After abdicating in 1814 and being exiled to Elba, he returned briefly before his final defeat at Waterloo in 1815. He spent his last years on the island of Saint Helena, where he died in 1821. His lasting legacy includes the Napoleonic Code, a legal system that influenced many countries.

Traits that give them away

  • Emperor. He ruled France as Emperor, so royalty and leadership questions are yes.
  • Military command. He is famous as a general and strategist.
  • Reform. The Napoleonic Code is one of his most enduring achievements.
  • Exile. He was exiled twice, first to Elba and then to Saint Helena.
  • The legend of his height. He was around average height for his time, despite the popular myth that he was very short.

How a round usually runs

Napoleon's round is a leadership round. Answer yes to real, yes to ruling and yes to military, and the engine is already close. The questions that follow are about era and country, which is where the round can drift towards other commanders of the same period.

Easy to confuse with

Napoleon is usually confused with other conquerors and rulers such as Julius Caesar, Alexander the Great, Charlemagne, and Louis XIV. Time period and country are the best separators: Napoleon is French, early 19th century, and strongly associated with Waterloo.

When to pick Napoleon Bonaparte

Pick Napoleon when you want a ruler round with a strong military shape. He is a good choice if you want to test whether the engine can separate a head of state from a general, since he was both.

Answers that go wrong

The hero question is the one that causes trouble. He is a national figure in France and a conqueror elsewhere, so the answer depends on the reputation the question is asking about. A confident answer either way can conflict with the other answers.
