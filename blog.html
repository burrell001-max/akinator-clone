The MindGenie AI Blog

Updated 27 November 2026

These are working notes rather than news. The site is a guessing game with a small, fixed roster and a scoring engine behind it, and most of what is interesting about it happens in the space between a question and an answer. The articles here explain that space: how the engine chooses what to ask, why some characters resist a guess, how to answer when you are not certain, and where the twenty-questions format came from.

They are written for players first. If you have ever finished a round and wondered why it took twelve questions instead of seven, or why the Genie offered a character you have never heard of, one of these pieces probably answers it. They are also written to be accurate about the machinery: the numbers and rules described here are the ones the site actually runs on, not a simplified version invented for a blog post.

Start with these

If you only read one article, read the one on what happens inside a round. It explains the roster, the scoring, the question choice and the stopping rule in order, and most of the other questions follow from it. If you are about to play with someone who keeps losing, the piece on the middle answers is usually the one that helps.

New pieces are added when there is something worth explaining, not on a schedule. Every article carries a publish date and is revised when the game changes underneath it.

How these notes are written

Each piece starts from something the game actually does. The rules described here were checked against the code that runs the site rather than against a general idea of how guessing games are supposed to work: the question limits, the stopping rule and the five answer options are the real ones. Where something is a judgement rather than a fact, the article says so.

That also means the articles can go stale. If a rule changes, the piece that described it is corrected at the same time, and the publish date stays as it was while the article itself is revised. Nothing here is written to fill a calendar, and there is no schedule.
