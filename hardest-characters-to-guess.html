Characters That Stump the Genie: 22 Hard Picks From the Roster

By Kent B Williams · Published 3 August 2026 · About the writer

Every character on this page is one the Genie can actually be asked to guess. That is the point of it: a list of hard picks is only useful if the engine knows them, and plenty of famous characters are not in the roster at all.

The site groups its fifty characters into three difficulty bands, and the twenty-two below are the hard one. A character lands here when their trait answers sit close to another character's. That makes the round long rather than impossible: the engine still gets there, but it needs most of its fourteen questions and one careless answer can send it to the neighbour.

This list is drawn from the category pages, which is where the bands are published: the movie, television, anime, video game and historical pages each split their characters into easy, medium and hard. If a character appears below, their card on that page sits under Hard, and the card also names the lookalike most likely to pull a round off course.

The overlaps are not accidents of the data. They come from fiction itself: wizards share staffs and beards, soldiers share equipment and training, rulers share thrones and wars. The engine has to work through the archetype before it can reach the person, and that work is what a long round feels like from the inside.

Movies

  1. Batman — a billionaire with no powers and a gadget for everything, which is the same answer set as Iron Man.
  2. Elsa — magic and royalty in one character, so she overlaps Maleficent on both counts.
  3. Deadpool — costumed and powered, and he matches Spider-Man until the questions turn to tone.
  4. Gandalf — an old wizard with a staff, which is also the description of Dumbledore.
  5. Frodo Baggins — small, powerless and not human, so most of his useful answers are negatives.
  6. Katniss Everdeen — a human with a weapon and no powers, which overlaps Trinity and Furiosa.
  7. Cinderella — human and unpowered with a royal ending, close to Elsa and Moana on the early answers.
  8. Harry Potter — a young wizard with a wand, which is half of Hermione's description.
  9. Hermione Granger — an unpowered human with magic at school, so the round needs role and personality questions.
  10. Trinity — a human fighter in a technological world, overlapping Furiosa on one side and Snake on the other.
  11. Furiosa — a human fighter in a desert, which is Trinity and Katniss with the setting changed.

TV

  1. Walter White — human, powerless and criminal, sharing a setting with the next name on this list.
  2. Saul Goodman — a lawyer in the same story, so the early answers fit both of them.
  3. Sherlock Holmes — a human detective with no powers, which looks like Batman until the costume question arrives.

Anime

  1. Goku — a powered fighter, so the strength questions fit Thor and several game leads before they fit him.

Video games

  1. Samus Aran — an armoured science-fiction lead, the same shape as Solid Snake and Kratos.
  2. Solid Snake — a soldier with no powers and a lot of equipment, which is the crowded end of the roster.
  3. Pikachu — a non-human mascot with electrical powers, so species and setting do the separating.

Historical figures

  1. Joan of Arc — a real, young, unpowered fighter, which is Mulan with the fictional answer changed.
  2. Nikola Tesla — a real inventor whose era and field questions arrive late in a round.
  3. Abraham Lincoln — a real leader, overlapping Cleopatra and Genghis Khan on role.
  4. Cleopatra — a real ruler, so leadership questions fit her and several others equally.

Why the famous ones are not here

Spider-Man, Mulan, The Doctor and the other characters in the easy band are absent for a good reason: they are guessed in a handful of questions, so recommending them as challenges would be misleading. The same goes for anything not in the roster. A character the engine cannot reach is not a hard pick, it is a dead end.

One warning before the list is used in a competition: these names are chosen for overlap, not for obscurity. Every one of them is a character most players can answer for without looking anything up, which is what makes a long round a fair test of answering rather than a test of memory.

How to use this list in a round

A hard pick is not automatically a good one. The characters here are hard because they sit close to a neighbour in the engine's data, which means a single uncertain answer can move the round to that neighbour. If you want a long round that still resolves, choose one of these and answer carefully rather than quickly.

The list also works in the other direction. If a character here is guessed quickly and correctly, that tells you their traits are more distinctive than the band suggests. If the same character is missed again and again, that is a sign the data needs adjusting rather than the player.

For a group round, let the person holding the secret choose from the list in private. The names are spread across films, television, anime, games and history so that no single category decides who wins.
