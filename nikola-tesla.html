🔌 Nikola Tesla

Nikola Tesla was an inventor and engineer whose work on alternating current helped create the modern electrical power system. He is remembered as one of the great minds of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Why the Genie sometimes needs a few extra questions

Tesla is a medium pick because he overlaps with other scientists such as Einstein and Edison. Questions about science, a real person, and the late 1800s help the Genie narrow things down.

Background on Nikola Tesla

Tesla was born in 1856 in Smiljan, in what was then the Austrian Empire and is now Croatia. He moved to the United States in 1884 and briefly worked for Thomas Edison before the two parted ways. His alternating current induction motor and polyphase system were licensed by George Westinghouse and used to build large-scale power networks, including the one at Niagara Falls.

He also invented the Tesla coil and experimented with wireless power at his Wardenclyffe Tower on Long Island. He died in 1943 in a New York hotel room. The unit of magnetic flux density, the tesla, is named after him, as is the car company founded decades later.

Traits that give them away

  • Inventor and engineer. His work centers on electricity and technology.
  • A real person. He was a historical figure, so the fictional question is a no.
  • Genius-level intellect. He is often seen as an archetype of the eccentric genius.
  • A famous rival. His competition with Edison is often called the War of the Currents.
  • Not a ruler. He held no political office.

Easy to confuse with

Tesla is most often confused with Albert Einstein, Thomas Edison, and Isaac Newton. Einstein is a physicist who developed relativity, while Edison is the inventor of the practical light bulb and phonograph. Tesla's focus on alternating current and his Croatian-American background are the best separators.
