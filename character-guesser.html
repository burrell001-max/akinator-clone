Character Guesser: Movies, TV, Anime, Games and History

MindGenie AI is a character guesser: you pick someone from a story or from history, and the Genie tries to work out who it is from a short series of questions. This page is a map of what you can pick from and how to choose a character that makes the game interesting.

🔮 Start guessing

Browse by category

  • Movie characters: superheroes, villains and franchise icons such as Darth Vader and Iron Man.
  • TV show characters: from Walter White to Michael Scott.
  • Anime characters: including SpongeBob and Levi Ackerman.
  • Video game characters: such as Solid Snake, Kratos and Samus Aran.
  • Historical figures: like Cleopatra, Nikola Tesla and Joan of Arc.

What makes a character easy or hard to guess

The Genie does best with characters that have clear, widely agreed traits: a distinctive costume, a well-known role, a famous setting. It struggles more with characters whose traits are ambiguous or shared with many others, such as supporting characters, anti-heroes whose morality is debated, or people from crowded historical periods. Each character guide on this site rates how tricky that pick tends to be and lists the traits the Genie will probably ask about.

Picking a character that makes the game fun

  • Choose someone you know well enough to answer questions confidently.
  • If you want a quick win, pick an icon like Batman or Pikachu.
  • If you want a challenge, pick someone with a twist, such as a villain who looks like a hero.

Real or fictional, either works

You aren't limited to made-up characters. Scientists, rulers and explorers are all fair game. The only rule is that you have to be able to describe them with simple traits.

What the categories actually contain

Each category page lists only characters that are in the engine's roster, and it groups them by how far each one sits from its nearest neighbour in the trait data. That is why some famous names appear in the hard group while less famous names appear in the easy group: fame helps a player answer consistently, but it is not what makes a character easy to separate. A very famous character who closely resembles another very famous character is still a difficult guess.

The guides are uneven on purpose. A character with a long, heavily adapted history — a comic hero with a dozen film versions, a detective with a century of reboots — needs a longer explanation than a character who appears in one series, because the version question changes the answers.

Choosing between the five categories

If you are new, pick a film character. Film characters tend to have a single defining look and a clear role, and the answers are easier to keep consistent. Television is the next step: a long-running series gives its characters time to change, which makes the version question matter more. Anime and video games reward players who know a specific series well, because the questions about species, powers and equipment carry more weight there. History is the odd one out: every historical figure answers no to the fictional question, and the round becomes a matter of era and role.

Frequently asked questions

How many characters can the Genie guess?

The engine carries a roster of fifty, split across films, television, anime, video games and history. The guides cover the ones with a page on the site.

Why do some categories look small?

Because the category pages list only characters the engine can really guess. A longer list of characters it cannot recognise would be less useful.

Can I suggest a character that is missing?

Yes. The contact page explains what to send, and suggestions are collected rather than answered one by one.

Which category is easiest?

Films, in general. Their characters have the clearest visual identities, which makes the costume and role questions more useful.
