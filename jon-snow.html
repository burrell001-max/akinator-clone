🐺 Jon Snow

Jon Snow is a central figure in a sprawling fantasy epic known for political intrigue, a large ensemble cast, and a long-running mystery around his parentage. He's frequently cited as one of the most recognizable characters in modern fantasy television.

Why the Genie usually gets this one

A fantasy setting, a specific role (member of a wall-guarding order, later a claimant to power), and a very well-known personal mystery make Jon Snow easy to isolate. The Genie mostly needs to confirm the fantasy setting and lead-character status before converging quickly.

Background on Jon Snow

Jon Snow is a central character in A Song of Ice and Fire, the fantasy novels by George R.R. Martin, and in HBO's Game of Thrones, where he is played by Kit Harington. He is raised at Winterfell, the seat of House Stark, as the apparent illegitimate son of Lord Eddard Stark, and he joins the Night's Watch, the order that guards the great Wall at the northern edge of the realm.

He is known for his sense of honor, his direwolf Ghost, and his Valyrian steel sword Longclaw. His story involves the threat of the White Walkers, a rise to leadership, and a complicated relationship with Daenerys Targaryen. His arc includes some of the most talked-about twists in the story, so we will avoid spoilers here.

Traits that give them away

  • Fantasy setting. Westeros is a medieval-style world with dragons and ancient magic.
  • A sworn brotherhood. The Night's Watch is a defined organization.
  • A swordsman. He is known for his sword and fighting skills.
  • A direwolf. Ghost is his animal companion.
  • Books and show. Because he appears in both, the book question depends on which version you imagine.

How a round usually runs

Jon Snow is a character whose round depends heavily on how much of the story you have in mind. Early on he is an outsider who joins a northern order; later he is a commander, then something else again. The engine handles the basics well and struggles when the answers mix the beginning and the end.

Easy to confuse with

Jon Snow is often mixed up with other fantasy heroes such as Aragorn from The Lord of the Rings, with other members of his family such as Ned or Robb Stark, and with Daenerys Targaryen. The Night's Watch and the direwolf are the clearest signals.

Tips for playing as Jon Snow

Decide whether you are thinking of the book Jon or the television Jon, since some details differ. If you want a challenge, pick another Stark. If you want a quick win, keep your answers focused on the Wall and the Night's Watch.

When to pick Jon Snow

Use Jon when you want a round built on setting and role rather than powers. He is a good pick for a player who knows one version of the story better than another, because it makes the version question concrete.

Answers that go wrong

The leadership question is where it goes wrong. He ends up leading, but he begins with no rank at all, and answering yes from the start contradicts the early questions. Answer for the point in the story you are picturing.

Frequently asked questions

Does Jon Snow have powers?

No. He is a human character, and the powers question is a no even in the parts of the story that feel supernatural.

Why does the Genie suggest other characters from the same series?

Is he a king?

No. His story involves command and a claim, but he is not a reigning monarch in the sense the royalty question means.

Which version should I answer for?

Whichever you know. The books and the series diverge, so mixing them produces answers that fit neither.
