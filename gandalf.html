Gandalf: A Character Guide for MindGenie AI

By Kent B Williams · Published November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Gandalf is a wizard with a beard, a staff and a long memory, which is a description that fits several characters in this roster. He is hard to guess for a specific reason: the answers that identify him are shared with Albus Dumbledore, and the questions that would separate them are the last ones the engine reaches.

Why the Genie needs a few extra questions

Gandalf answers yes to fictional, yes to powers, yes to a staff or weapon, and yes to old. Those four answers describe a whole archetype rather than a person, and the archetype includes Dumbledore, Maleficent and several other magic users.

The round has to establish his role as a guide rather than a schoolmaster, and his setting as a journey rather than a school, before the answer becomes clear. Both of those are role questions, and role questions come late.

Who he is

Gandalf is one of the Istari, a group sent into Middle-earth to oppose a rising power. He arrives as an old man with a staff and a temper, works mostly by persuasion and timing, and is present at the start of two journeys that decide the fate of the world.

The detail that matters for a guessing game is that his age and appearance are a form rather than a fact. He looks like an old man because that is the shape he was sent in.

Traits that give him away

  • Fictional: Yes. He comes from Tolkien's legendarium.
  • Powers: Yes. Magic is central to the character.
  • Human: Complicated. He looks human but is not one by origin.
  • Old: Yes. His apparent age is a defining feature.
  • Uses a staff: Yes. Along with a sword.
  • Role: A guide and mentor rather than a protagonist.

How a round usually runs

The first few questions settle the obvious facts: fictional, powerful, old, bearded. From there the round spends several questions inside the wizard cluster, which is where Gandalf and Dumbledore are hard to tell apart.

The questions that resolve him are about role and setting: a mentor on a journey rather than a headmaster of a school. Once those arrive, the round ends quickly.

Where players get tripped up

The human question is the usual trap. If you answer by appearance, Gandalf is human; if you answer by origin, he is not. Decide which reading you are answering for before the round starts, and stay with it.

The second trap is Dumbledore. Answering yes to school or teaching pulls the engine towards him, because that is the difference between the two characters in trait terms.

Tips for a round with Gandalf

  • Answer yes on powers: Magic is the most useful fact about him.
  • Be consistent on human: Appearance or origin, but not both.
  • Answer no on teaching: He advises; he does not run a school.
  • Say yes on journey or quest: It separates him from the other wizard.

When to pick Gandalf

Gandalf is a good hard pick for a mixed group, because almost everyone knows him and almost nobody can answer the human question without hesitating. That hesitation is the point: it shows how much of a round depends on reading a question the same way the engine does.
